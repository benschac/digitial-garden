## Motivation

Richard Hamming’s _You and Your Research_ is a talk I really like, and one I think is worth sharing. It asks a question that reaches beyond research: how do you choose work worth doing?

Hamming distinguishes working hard from choosing where to put that effort. His challenge is to ask what the important problems in your field are, and whether the work you’re doing might lead toward them. That feels like a useful question to keep close, even when the next step is a small experiment rather than a grand project.

You can [read the transcript of Hamming’s 1986 talk](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html) or [watch You and Your Research on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a1zDuOPkMSw).

## Learning

Another idea from Hamming’s talk is that knowledge compounds. Understanding something today gives you more to build on tomorrow. Small amounts of sustained effort can change which questions you’re able to ask, not just how quickly you can answer them.

I want this site to leave room for that process: projects, questions, and explanations that can get better as I learn more. The [blog](/blog) holds the longer write-ups; the [playground](/playground) holds experiments.
